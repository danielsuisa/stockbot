## Known biases

- Survivorship: symbols are matched through today's SEC ticker list and Yahoo only serves live tickers, so delisted and renamed stocks are missing (see coverage). The baseline comes from the same covered stocks, so the lift is like-for-like; the absolute hit rates are not.
- Multi-class issuers and splits between the SEC figure and the FINRA date add noise to the short-interest %.
- Yahoo bad ticks can create or hide a hit; the picks CSV lets any hit be checked by hand.

## Post-run check, not part of the verdict: squeeze signal or volatility filter?

The verdict compares picks with *every* eligible stock-day. Most of those are calm large caps that almost never
move 50 % in two weeks. So each pick is also compared with eligible stock-days of the **same 20-session realized
volatility** (same period). The check counts both a +50 % spike (a hit) and a symmetric −33 % crash (low ≤ open ÷ 1.5)
inside the same 10 sessions. Script: `docs/backtest/squeeze-2026-09-27-volmatched.py` (reads the same cache).

**What this measures (out-of-sample 2024 → 2026):**

- Against stocks that are just as volatile, the frozen list's +50 % rate is 2.1× (6.8 % vs 3.2 %). Its −33 % crash
  rate is 1.85× (7.1 % vs 3.8 %). Spikes and crashes are about **equally likely** for a pick (6.8 % vs 7.1 %). The
  list mainly finds stocks about to move far in either direction: volatility expansion, with at most a small upside
  tilt.
- Short interest adds a small tilt, not an edge: with the same trigger, SI ≥ 20 % vs SI < 5 % gives 6.8 % vs 5.8 %
  spikes and 6.9 % vs 8.2 % crashes. At these sample sizes (1,334 vs 5,097 picks) neither difference is statistically
  clear. The trigger-only list's higher raw lift (9.39×) comes from picking even more volatile names; its crash rate
  is 11.5 %.
- For options: no reliable directional edge is measured, so a plain call bet on a pick is not supported by this
  data. The signal is about coming volatility, which option prices (IV) on these names usually already charge for.
  Free historical IV is not available, so whether realized volatility beats implied volatility is **not measured**.
