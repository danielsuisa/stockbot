## Known biases

- Survivorship: symbols are matched through today's SEC ticker list and Yahoo only serves live tickers, so delisted and renamed stocks are missing (see coverage). The baseline comes from the same covered stocks, so the lift is like-for-like; the absolute hit rates are not.
- Multi-class issuers and splits between the SEC figure and the FINRA date add noise to the short-interest %.
- Yahoo bad ticks can create or hide a hit; the picks CSV lets any hit be checked by hand.

## Post-run check, not part of the verdict: is it a squeeze signal or a volatility filter?

The verdict compares picks with *every* eligible stock-day, and most of those are calm large caps that almost never
move 50 % in two weeks. So each pick is also compared with eligible stock-days of the **same 20-session realized
volatility** (same period), for a +50 % spike (hit) and for a symmetric −33 % crash (low ≤ open ÷ 1.5) inside the
same 10 sessions. Script: `docs/backtest/squeeze-2026-09-27-volmatched.py` (reads the same cache).

**What this measures (out-of-sample 2024 → 2026):**

- Against stocks that are just as volatile, the frozen list's +50 % rate is 1.9× (7.4 % vs 3.9 %), but its −33 % crash
  rate is also 1.85× and larger in absolute terms (9.2 % vs 5.0 %). The list finds stocks about to move far in
  **either** direction: a volatility-expansion signal, not a directional squeeze signal.
- Short interest adds little beyond the volume/momentum trigger: with the same trigger, SI ≥ 20 % vs SI < 5 % gives
  7.4 % vs 7.0 % spikes and 9.3 % vs 10.8 % crashes. The trigger-only list's higher raw lift (9.26×) comes from
  picking even more volatile names (its crash rate is 14.2 %).
- For options: no directional edge is measured, so a plain call bet on a pick is not supported by this data. The signal
  is about coming volatility, which option prices (IV) on these names usually already charge for; free historical IV
  is not available, so whether realized volatility beats implied volatility is **not measured**.
